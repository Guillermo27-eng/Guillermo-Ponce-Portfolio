## My story

I grew up in South Central Los Angeles, and for first through eighth grade I made the trip to Boyle Heights every day to attend Dolores Mission School. My mother was raising me and my siblings, and school and church were where I spent most of my childhood. It was there, at a young age, that I learned the Golden Rule: treat people the way you want to be treated.

Dolores Mission is a Jesuit parish, and its history explains a lot about how I think. In the 1980s it was one of the poorest parishes in Los Angeles, in a neighborhood with some of the worst gang violence in the city, and many of the families in the pews were refugees from El Salvador. The Jesuits took over the parish, and in 1986 a young priest named Father Greg Boyle became its pastor. At a time when the city's answer to gang violence was suppression and prison, he did something much simpler. He went to the people everyone else was running from, stayed, and started giving them jobs. In 1988 he and the parish began what became Homeboy Industries, which now trains and employs thousands of people each year.

He calls it radical kinship: you do not fix people from a distance, you stand with them. The Jesuits have a name for the same idea, *cura personalis*, care for the whole person. I did not read either one in a book. I grew up inside it. Where I am from, we were on the wrong side of a lot of statistics, and the people who studied us rarely lived anywhere near us. Father Greg's answer was never to study us harder. It was to show up and stay.

That is the thread through everything I have done since. I worked as the community organizing lead at LA Voice, leading engagement for the Home Is Sacred campaign on housing and tenant rights in East Los Angeles and Boyle Heights. I spent a year at East Los Angeles College, then transferred to USC, where I am earning a Bachelor of Science in Public Policy & Data Analytics and a Master of Public Administration at the same time. I wanted the data skills and the community work to grow together, not one after the other.

So far that has meant building GIS frameworks that informed $700M in climate investment decisions for South LA, modeling a decade of Medicare spending at the USC Schaeffer Center, and building the systems behind communications work for some of the most visible public agencies in Los Angeles.

I am still early in this. What I know is that the work lives in two places at once, in the data and in the room, and I am learning to move between both. I do it as a bilingual, first-generation son of South Central, and I intend to keep working for the neighborhoods that policy too often decides about without them.

**Focus areas:** Public Policy · Data Analytics · Health Policy · Urban Resilience · GIS Mapping · Strategic Communications

## Featured projects

### Urban Resilience Index: Baldwin Hills & Urban Watersheds Conservancy
*USC Public Exchange · [Project folder](research/urban-resilience-index)*

**The problem.** The Conservancy had grown from 2 to 70 square miles and gained access to $700M in Prop 4 funding, but needed a better tool to know where to spend it.

**What I did.** I scored each indicator in the Urban Resilience Index's five categories (Community Empowerment, Water Management, Biodiversity, Extreme Heat & Fire, Park Access) on accessibility, transparency, and comprehensiveness. I then compared it against three outside GIS tools: the Nature Conservancy's Resilient Land Mapping Tool, the Connecticut Environmental Justice Mapping Tool, and the Living Infrastructure Field Kit.

| Stage | Tool | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Spatial analysis | ArcGIS Pro | Scored high-vulnerability zones across 5 resilience categories |
| Tool comparison | Structured rubric | Side-by-side evaluation of 3 GIS platforms |
| Reporting | Excel dashboards | Funding utilization reports for municipal and nonprofit partners |

**Result.** Four sets of recommendations (data improvements, interface enhancements, user accessibility, and future development) to inform how the Index guides $700M in climate investment across South LA.

`ArcGIS Pro` `GIS Analysis` `Climate Equity` `Urban Resilience` `Land-Use Planning` `Policy Writing`

### Not All Dementia Care Is Equal
*USC Schaeffer Center for Health Policy & Economics, Undergraduate Research Associate · [Project folder](research/dementia-medicare-analysis)*

**The question.** Medicare spends roughly $10,000 more per year on patients with dementia than on comparable patients without, but the gap looks very different depending on the state.

**What I did.** I analyzed Medicare Fee-for-Service claims from 2011 to 2021 across 8 states and 9 service categories. The states were chosen for demographic and policy range, from high-spending states (CA, NY, IL) to rural, resource-constrained ones (MS, GA).

| Stage | Tool | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Data cleaning | Stata | Structured claims for 8 states, controlled for age, sex, race |
| Analysis | Stata, Excel pivot tables | Per-capita spending by state, year, and dementia status |
| Visualization | Stacked bar charts | State-level spending trends, 2011 vs. 2021 |
| Presentation | Research poster and slides | Findings presented to 30+ Schaeffer Center associates |

**Key findings**
- Mississippi's dementia spending gap narrowed from about $11K to $4.5K, while New York's widened to about $11K by 2021.
- Inpatient and skilled nursing costs dominated in both years, while Carrier and Part D spending grew in most states by 2021.
- CA and NY spent 15 to 35% more on average, and some states differed by nearly 50%.

`Stata` `Excel` `Medicare Claims Analysis` `Health Policy` `Data Visualization` `Quantitative Research`

### From Poverty to Gang Involvement
*USC Suzanne Dworak-Peck School of Social Work, SOWK 350 · [Project folder](policy-analysis/gang-trauma-policy)*

**The argument.** In some LA County neighborhoods child poverty reaches 68%, and the path from poverty to gang affiliation runs through systemic trauma, not individual failure.

Historic structural inequalities → intergenerational trauma → institutional distrust → gang vulnerability

**What I did.** I led a policy analysis of how redlining, mass incarceration, and disinvestment create that pathway for LA County youth, with recommendations at three levels and a full cost-benefit analysis.

| Level | Recommendation | Estimated cost |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | Trauma-informed family therapy in all LAHSA family shelters | Included in hub budget |
| Mezzo | Family Resilience Hubs co-locating GRYD, DMH, and YDD services | About $800K per hub per year |
| Macro | Countywide reform: multi-year contracts and cross-agency coordination | Policy-level |

**Why it matters.** Diverting one youth from incarceration saves about $300K per year. Higher school completion raises lifetime earnings by $630K to $900K. Trauma-informed care reduces ER and hospital use by 30 to 50%.

`Policy Analysis` `Social Justice Research` `Cost-Benefit Analysis` `Community Violence Intervention` `Program Evaluation`

## Professional experience

### Lee Andrews Group
**Strategic Communications Intern** · [Full case study](professional-experience/lee-andrews-group)

A public affairs firm managing 50+ client proposals across LA County kept its work in emails, scattered documents, and individual memory. Before building anything, I surveyed the whole firm about how work actually moved, and every person responded. The answers shaped what I built.

- **The LAG Project Hub (Airtable):** a firm-wide operating system that tracks projects, tasks, meetings and notes, and the staff directory, with intake forms, role-based views for project managers and individual staff, and automated email notifications when work is assigned and when it is finished. I presented it to the whole firm, ran a live demo, and wrote the plan for the next phase: project managers owning task status, event staffing moved into Airtable, and training sessions to drive adoption.
- **An automated RFP finder:** three scheduled runs a day search Southern California procurement sources, flag opportunities that match the firm's service lines and priority agencies, and log them in Airtable for the business development team.
- **Performance analytics:** a reporting system that compares billable hours from timesheets against individual billable goals, to help decide who should take on more project work without going over a client's monthly budget.
- **Client work in Airtable:** outreach trackers, survey dashboards and a stakeholder tracking base for community engagement projects, including an affordable housing outreach project and a community health screening outreach effort with a 167-location database.
- **Reporting:** weekly Google Analytics reports that turned transit agency program performance into insight for real estate development and community violence intervention programs.

**Result:** about a 90% improvement in operational efficiency by cutting back-and-forth communication and giving leadership a live view of client work.

`Airtable` `Workflow Automation` `Survey Design` `Google Analytics` `Stakeholder Coordination` `Stakeholder Engagement`
