# USC Student Basic Needs Office

*Data Strategy Lead and Undergraduate Operations Assistant · December 2024 to May 2026 · Los Angeles, California*

The Student Basic Needs Office supports USC students facing food and resource insecurity through the Trojan Food Pantry, Tommy's Closet (a free, donation-based professional attire event), and supplemental meal swipes. I held two roles at once: I worked the operations side, restocking the pantry and helping run Tommy's Closet, and I led the data side.

## The problem
The office was running at a resource deficit and had no consistent way to see where resources were going. More than five student service programs tracked their work differently, or not at all. Leadership asked for more transparency in how students were being served, and nobody could say what was being used, what was running low, or where to move money.

## What I did

| Stage | Tool | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnose | Operational audits | Pulled weekly purchase receipts into Excel and analyzed spending trends and inventory reductions across food pantry stations in 3+ audits |
| Standardize | Excel tracking dashboard | Standardized weekly data collection across 5+ programs so every program reported the same way |
| Report | Utilization reports | Delivered recurring, transparent reports to senior leadership to realign capital allocation with student demand |
| Engage | Student and partner outreach | Gathered feedback from the students using the programs and the staff running them, and used it to shape outreach and make the programs more visible |

I also learned to tell the same data in different ways: for a student deciding whether to use a service, and for leadership deciding where to direct resources.

## What came out of it
Over the 2025-26 academic year, the office saw growth across its programs:

| Measure | Change |
|---|---|
| Trojan Food Pantry visits | +59.3% |
| Volunteer hours | +117.2% |
| Tommy's Closet visits | +21.0% |
| Total engagement and visits (as of May 8) | 14,591 |

These are office-wide results, and outreach and volunteers contributed alongside the reporting. What the data work did was close the reporting gap, give leadership a clear weekly picture, and move spending toward actual student demand.

## What it taught me
The spreadsheets were never the point. Every gap in the data was a student who did not get what they needed that week. That is why I pair measurement with engagement in everything I build.
