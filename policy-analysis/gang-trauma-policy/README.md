# From Poverty to Gang Involvement

*USC Suzanne Dworak-Peck School of Social Work · SOWK 350*

## The argument
In some LA County neighborhoods, child poverty reaches 68%. The path from that poverty to gang affiliation runs through systemic trauma, not individual failure.

Historic structural inequalities → intergenerational trauma → institutional distrust → gang vulnerability

## What I did
I led a policy analysis of how redlining, mass incarceration, and community disinvestment create that pathway for youth in Los Angeles County. I evaluated existing programs, including GRYD and trauma-focused therapy models, and developed recommendations at three levels with a cost-benefit analysis.

| Level | Recommendation | Estimated cost |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | Trauma-informed family therapy in all LAHSA family shelters | Included in hub budget |
| Mezzo | Family Resilience Hubs co-locating GRYD, DMH, and YDD services | About $800K per hub per year |
| Macro | Countywide reform with multi-year contracts and cross-agency coordination | Policy-level |

## Why it matters
Diverting one youth from incarceration saves about $300K per year. Higher school completion raises lifetime earnings by $630K to $900K. Trauma-informed care reduces emergency room and hospital use by 30 to 50%.
